# Draft captions for Figures 1–8

**Figure 1. Who and when.** (a) In a seeded schematic grid of 8 patients by 21 days, filled circles mark events; the outlined row isolates which days are risky (when), and the outlined column isolates which patient is at risk (who). Patient event rates were drawn uniformly from 10% to 50% (seed 20261013; realized rates range from 13% to 41%). The grid has 36 events and 132 event-free days, forming 4,752 event–event-free pairs: 574 compare days from the same patient (12.1%), 202 compare different patients on the same day (4.3%), and 3,976 are other pairs (83.7%). (b) Ben has events on days 1, 3, 4, 7, and 9 of 10 (whole-record rate 50%); Anna has an event on day 4 of 10 (10%). Their 84 pooled pairs include 50 between-patient comparisons (45 wins and 5 losses) and 34 within-patient ties. The pooled AUC is 62/84 = 0.74; each per-patient AUC is 0.50.

**Figure 2. Finite-record bias.** (a) A real forecast uses earlier days and leaves the scored day out; the whole-record rate includes the scored day. (b) For Clara’s 10-day record, events occur on days 2, 5, and 9. Forecasts averaged over the other 9 days are 22% on event days and 33% on event-free days; the separate AUC glyph shows score 0 against baseline 0.5. (c) The rate-so-far forecasts on the 3 event days are 0%, 25%, and 25%; forecasts on event-free days range from 29% to 50%. Day 1 is unscored; the AUC over the remaining 9 days is 0 against the 0.50 baseline. Arrows connect event days 2, 5, and 9 to the next day’s forecast rise. (d) For the flipped rate-so-far forecast under 30% independent daily event risk, AUCs at 10, 30, 100, 300, 1,000, 3,000, and 5,000 scored days are 0.704, 0.640, 0.582, 0.548, 0.528, 0.516, and 0.513; the rate-so-far mirror is shown as a second curve. Valid replication counts are 2,435 at 10 days, 2,499 at 30 days, and 2,500 at each longer record length; whiskers show MCSE.

**Figure 3. Record length and the two dials.** (a) The expanding rate-so-far AUC is shown for independent daily event rates of 5%, 15%, and 30% at equally spaced record lengths of 60, 100, 180, 365, and 730 days. (b) The A3 grid compares the share of pooled event–event-free pairs from the same patient with the black dashed 1/K reference. (c) It shows mean per-patient AUC for the same rate-so-far forecast at K = 4, 8, 16, 32, and 100 patients and record lengths T = 10, 20, 40, 80, and 365 days (25 cells; 4,000 replications per cell). Error bars are omitted; Monte Carlo standard errors are below 0.002.

**Figure 4. Four errors.** The 2 × 2 schematic separates between-patient bias from finite-record bias and shows whether each error moves the score or the baseline. Error 1 raises the score (dot up and right); error 2 shifts the baseline either down or up; error 3 lowers the score (dot down and left, in the looks-worse colour); error 4 raises the baseline (ring above the no-skill score, in the looks-worse colour). Error 1 affects AUC, average precision (AP), sensitivity + specificity − 1, PPV, NPV, and calibration slope. Error 2 affects AP, PPV, NPV, net benefit, and Brier skill. Error 3 affects AUC, AP, sensitivity + specificity − 1, PPV, NPV, net benefit, Brier skill, and calibration slope. Error 4 affects net benefit and Brier skill. The glyph directions are schematic and do not encode shift magnitude.

**Figure 5. The shuffled baseline.** One simulated patient has 34 events in 100 days (30% event probability; seed 20261012); the first day has no rate-so-far forecast, leaving 99 scored days. The observed AUC is 0.330, the mean of 200 shuffled AUCs is 0.421, and the excess is −0.091. The record panel shows the observed order and 3 example shuffles as a four-row raster; each shuffled forecast is rebuilt from its shuffled history. The glyph above the histogram shows the observed score, shuffled baseline, and their excess.

**Figure 6. Result 1: the shuffled rate so far.** CTN-0094 results are shown for 246 test patients and 4,237 scored rows, with 200 within-patient shuffles (seed 20261009). Rows show AUC, average precision, PPV, NPV, sensitivity + specificity − 1, net benefit, Brier skill, and calibration slope. Columns show Pooled, Per-patient, and Shuffled-baseline scoring. Filled dots are scores, open rings are baselines, and colored segments show excess; filled and open triangles apply the same score/baseline encoding to the run-in rate. Small square and diamond markers show REPLACE-BG and College Experience (CES) scores on the AUC and calibration-slope rows.

**Figure 7. Result 2: the fitted model.** The same 9 measures and 3 scorings are shown for the fitted model in CTN-0094 (246 patients; 4,237 scored rows; 200 within-patient shuffles; seed 20261010). In the Shuffled-baseline panel, the dot and ring show the mean score and mean shuffled baseline. The separate excess column shows median excess and its interquartile range, except for the conditional within-patient slope row: its score is 0.671, its shuffled baseline is −0.177, and its conditional excess is 0.848 (p = 0.005).

**Figure 8. Result 3: ranking the closer pair.** Each point is the share of 500 replications in which A ranks above B by AUC for daily autocorrelation ρ = 0.4 versus ρ = 0.3. The equally spaced x-axis categories are record lengths of 30, 100, and 365 days. Panels cross low spread (REPLACE-BG) or high spread (CTN-0094) with 50 or 150 patients. Point colors show Pooled, Per-patient, and Shuffled-baseline scoring; the dashed line marks equal ranking at 0.5. Each replication uses 50 within-patient shuffles (seed 20261011).
